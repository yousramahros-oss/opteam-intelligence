## Editorial notes

What changed from the original draft, and why:

| Original | Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "we believe this comparison should be made on an equivalent basis" | Stacked hedges dilute the point | Direct request, one sentence |
| "a lower retainer may not necessarily reflect the same level of input" | Reads as criticising the competitor's price | "reflects a different scope rather than a better rate" — same point, no attack |
| "Marka brings strong technical expertise… Our strengths, meanwhile…" | Sets up a head-to-head comparison you can lose | Reframed as complementary — harder to argue with, and true |
| "AI supports our efficiency but does not replace the expertise" | Defensive against an accusation that may not have been made | Kept but neutralised, plus an offer to demonstrate |
| IP paragraph placed after the deadline | Lands as a threat attached to an ultimatum | Moved after the ask, softened, paired with a constructive offer (transfer on signature) |
| "We would be grateful to receive a decision…" | Passive; easy to ignore | Direct question with a date — requires a reply |
| "significant time and effort in meetings, research…" | Unquantified; reads as a claim, not evidence | Placeholder for specifics — numbers do the persuading |

### Before sending

1. **Fill in `[X weeks]` and the deliverables list.** Specifics carry the value argument: "3 months, 11 meetings, a market study, a positioning framework and two prototypes" is far stronger than "significant time and effort."
2. **Fill in `[date]`.** A deadline without a date is not a deadline. Give 5–7 working days.
3. **Decide on the AI paragraph.** Keep it only if the subject was actually raised. If nobody mentioned it, including it plants a doubt that wasn't there — cut it and the email is stronger.
4. **Check the IP clause against anything already signed** (NDA, letter of intent, purchase order). If a document already assigns IP, this paragraph contradicts it and should be removed.
