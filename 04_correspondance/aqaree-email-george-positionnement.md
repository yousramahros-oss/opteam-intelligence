## Editorial notes

What changed from the original draft, and why:

| Original | Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "we believe this comparison should be made on an equivalent basis" | Stacked hedges dilute the point | Direct request, one sentence |
| "a lower retainer may not necessarily reflect the same level of input" | Reads as criticising the competitor's price | "reflects a different scope rather than a better rate" — same point, no attack |
| "Marka brings strong technical expertise… Our strengths, meanwhile…" | Sets up a head-to-head comparison you can lose | Reframed as complementary — harder to argue with, and true |
| "AI supports our efficiency but does not replace the expertise" | Defensive against an accusation that may not have been made | Kept but neutralised, plus an offer to demonstrate |
| IP paragraph placed after the deadline | Lands as a threat attached to an ultimatum | Moved after the ask, softened, paired with a constructive offer (transfer on signature) |
| "We would be grateful to receive a decision…" | Passive; easy to ignore | Direct question with a date — requires a reply |
| "significant time and effort in meetings, research…" | Unquantified; reads as a claim, not evidence | Placeholder for specifics — numbers do the persuading |

### Before sending

1. **Fill in `[X weeks]` and the deliverables list.** Specifics carry the value argument: "3 months, 11 meetings, a market study, a positioning framework and two prototypes" is far stronger than "significant time and effort."
2. **Fill in `[date]`.** A deadline without a date is not a deadline. Give 5–7 working days.
3. **Decide on the AI paragraph.** Keep it only if the subject was actually raised. If nobody mentioned it, including it plants a doubt that wasn't there — cut it and the email is stronger.
4. **Check the IP clause against anything already signed** (NDA, letter of intent, purchase order). If a document already assigns IP, this paragraph contradicts it and should be removed.

**Subject: Aqaree — where we stand, and what we need to know**

The pricing we presented was already adjusted significantly, because this is Mohamed's project and we wanted to support its growth. The same scope and involvement would normally be priced higher. So where a proposal comes in lower, the useful question isn't whether it's cheaper — it's what sits inside it: how many strategic deliverables, at what depth, with what turnaround, and who actually does the work. That's a comparison I'm confident we hold up well in.

Marka has real strength in technology, tooling and website development — areas where your teams have expertise we don't claim. Marketing strategy, positioning, brand development, growth, content direction and go-to-market execution are ours. I'm saying all this with respect, George, and not to create tension. Ikrame and I have approached this throughout with one objective: what we genuinely believe is best for Aqaree and its growth potential. I'd simply like us to keep the same clarity and professionalism on both sides.

## Editorial notes — Version C

Lines removed from the long draft, and why they worked against us:

| Line | Problem |
|---|---|
| "I would be very interested to see whether another vendor can genuinely provide the same scope… at a lower price" | Reads as a dare. Invites George to go and prove us wrong — and he may come back saying they can. |
| "I would be curious to understand what has actually been included in those alternative proposals" | Asks to see a competitor's confidential quotation. George cannot grant this even if sympathetic, and asking suggests unfamiliarity with procurement norms. |
| "very few vendors would have already invested the amount of time… before the partnership was even fully operational" | Sunk cost. Buyers don't pay for a vendor's pre-sales investment; can read as "we over-invested, so we're owed." Reframed as forward value. |
| "The work we produce is not simply AI-generated content… created randomly" | Repeating an accusation in its harshest form amplifies it, even in denial. Five paragraphs compressed to one, stated positively, with an offer of proof. |
| "because we believed that we were building something meaningful together" | Past tense signals emotional withdrawal — undercuts the "we'll continue with the same commitment" offer two paragraphs later. |
| "I genuinely hope that the level of commitment… will also be recognized and respected" | Reproach. Asks George to feel something rather than decide something; puts him on the defensive. |
| "But I also think clarity works both ways." | Confrontational pivot in a message that otherwise wants a yes. Replaced with a plain request. |
| "Our time and expertise have value, just as yours do." | Implies theirs is not being respected. The rest of the email makes the point without saying it. |

so a skim reader can find the ask. Length 700 → 470 words.
